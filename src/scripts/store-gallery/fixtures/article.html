Why plain text outlives every app

By Ada Park · September 18, 2026 · 6 min read

For a decade I trusted my notes to whatever app was fashionable. Every few years the app shut down, changed its pricing, or quietly broke its export, and I was left holding a tangle of proprietary HTML.

Plain text is the only format that has never let me down. It opens everywhere, diffs cleanly in version control, and it will still open in thirty years. Markdown adds just enough structure — headings, links, lists — without locking anything in.

The formats that stuck

FormatSinceOpens?
Plain text1963Everywhere
Markdown2004Everywhere
Word .doc1997Usually
Note app DB2011Rarely

A back-of-the-envelope model

If a format loses tools at a steady rate λ, its half-life is:

t1/2=ln⁡2λ

For plain text, λ is effectively zero.

Keep it scriptable

Because the notes are just files, a few lines of code can index all of them:

from pathlib import Path

for note in sorted(Path("notes").glob("*.md")):
    words = len(note.read_text().split())
    print(f"{note.stem}: {words} words")

The trick was never a better app. It is keeping what you write in a form you can read without anyone's permission.

So clip the page, keep the Markdown, and let the apps come and go.
